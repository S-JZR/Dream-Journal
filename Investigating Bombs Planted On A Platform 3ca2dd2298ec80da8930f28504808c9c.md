I was on a bomb squad team investigating reports of bombs being planted on a small tall square metal platform on a river. The platform comprised multiple square parts connected together with a flat top surface, wires and pipes on the bottom, and ladders on each corner leading to catwalks beneath it. We landed on the top in a helicopter and split up looking for bombs anywhere we could see them. I descended the bottom-left ladder and found a stereotypical dynamite stick bomb with a red LED countdown timer showing 10 minutes nestled between pipes.

> **Me**: I found one under here.
> 

> **Man 1**: I also found one up here.
> 

> **Man 2**: There is one under here so that makes three bombs.
> 

A man wearing a yellow bomb vest exited from the helicopter and moved to address my bomb first.

> **Man 3**: There is only 6 minutes remaining I don’t think we can diffuse them all in time.
> 

> **Me**: Maybe we could use saws to cut off sections of the platform and move them far away.
> 

> **Man 3:** That’s good quick thinking.
> 

After radioing the commander to request for the immediate delivery of a saw there was no response

> **Man 3** (Radio): [Relaying the situation]
> 

I noticed red flares being dropped in the distance in an open field across the river with trees scattered around and an elevated dirt path on the left.

> **Me**: I think those might be supply drops but I'm not sure.
> 

We crossed the river as night fell and one of my team members was cautious.

> **Man 3**: Don’t move any closer. Use your thermal vision and make sure it’s safe.
> 

I looked through a thermal vision monocular and initially didn’t see anything but hundreds of white silhouettes of armed people emerged from the path. We realized that we would have to fight them to retrieve the supplies. I walked up to them, saw they were people dressed in black holding AK-47’s, and pulled out a machine gun to mow them down. We confirmed the flares were supply drops, retrieved the saws, and returned.

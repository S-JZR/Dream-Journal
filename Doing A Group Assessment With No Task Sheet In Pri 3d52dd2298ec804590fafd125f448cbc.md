I was a student in primary school that entered a small computer room with 1 row of 5 thick white computer monitors on a desk. 1 male student and 2 female substitute teachers were seated inside.

> **Woman**: [Me], this is who you will be working with on your group assessment.
> 

I sat in a chair beside the boy and looked at his screen.

> **Me**: Can you explain what the assessment is and what work you have already done.
> 

> **Boy**: Sure, I'll load the Word document I've been working on.
> 

He loaded a strange looking word document containing a dark blue background and an oval with a few lines of vague text on it. I felt concerned that he was clueless and half-assing it.

> **Boy**: The assessment is to explain why one classroom of our choosing is well designed.
> 

> **Me**: Is there a task sheet?
> 

> **Boy**: No.
> 

We walked outside to the eating area in year 7 block.

> **Me**: I’m concerned that since this assessment is so open ended that we risk going in circles and wasting our time. We should establish a clear objective so we stay focused. What if we wrote about all the shortcomings in accessibility throughout the school and suggested ways to fix them.
> 

> **Renee**: That’s a good idea.
> 

> **Boy**: Come on. You’re not just going to suggest putting ramps everywhere are you? Is the pool going to get a ramp?
> 

> **Me**: I’m serious, I think there is a lot of potential here. We could group them by feasibility and outline photos to show the placement of structures.
> 

I was focused on the Tuckshop area which had a gutter and I don’t think any ramps. I was thinking the ramp could run parallel with the stairs that went upstairs and have a hole through it for water to pass through.

I was walking down the road to my local shopping mall at night when I encountered an Asian man running a Japanese food stall on a metal table in the middle of the road. The table featured a stainless steel cooking pot without a lid on a burner and a wooden chopping board. The roads were completely devoid of cars. I approached the man to order food.

> **Me**: Can I please have some Teriyaki Chicken.
> 

> **Man 1**: Sure, just give me a moment.
> 

He handed me the food in a takeaway paper bowl and I thought it tasted amazing.

I continued walking up the road until I entered a math classroom at secondary school where Gavin and other students were seated at desks and a male teacher was standing beside someone to the left.

> **Me**: I just got some Teriyaki Chicken down the road and it tasted amazing.
> 

> **Man 2**: Maybe you should go back and get some for everyone else. Does anyone want to give $10 dollars for some?
> 

Various students extended bank notes towards me and I collected them. I was worried that something would go wrong and they would be disappointed in me because I didn't explain it was only a small food stall with limited supplied when they were probably expecting a typical restaurant so some people might miss out on getting anything. To avoid forgetting their orders, I recorded everyone’s names, how much money they gave, and what they wanted on a spiral-torn piece of paper.

I noticed a delivery bike with a box mounted to the back leaning against the wall in the classroom so I rode it back to the Asian man.

> **Me**: I brought you a list of orders, are you able to make them?
> 

> **Man 1**: Yeah, sure.
> 

While he was preparing the food he ran out of supplies as I expected and reduced the portion sizes. He handed over multiple plates with a single slice of orange cake on them.

> **Me**: Can’t you put more food on them?
> 

> **Man 1**: Sorry, I've run out so I have to ration it.
> 

I reluctantly stored the food in my bike’s delivery box and travelled back to my secondary school.
